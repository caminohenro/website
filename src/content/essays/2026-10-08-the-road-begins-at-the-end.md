I stand at Cape Finisterre — *Finis Terrae*, the End of the Earth — where medieval pilgrims once burned their clothes and watched the sun drown in the Atlantic. For centuries, this granite headland marked the boundary of the known world. Beyond the horizon: nothing. The end.

But instead of turning back toward Santiago, toward home, toward everything familiar, I turn east.

Fourteen thousand kilometers of road, mountain, desert, and sea lie between here and Shikoku, the smallest of Japan's main islands, where eighty-eight temples trace a circular path that pilgrims have walked for over a thousand years. The Japanese call such a pilgrim *henro* (遍路) — one who walks the path.

## Why Walk?

Most people look for security in their job, their career, their savings account. I understand the impulse. But somewhere along the way I began to suspect that real security can't be found in any of those things. It has to be something deeper — something that doesn't disappear when your employer decides you're no longer useful.

I thought of wild animals and ancient nomads. Their security is entirely dependent on their adaptations to the environment around them. Not on a single salary. Not on a single institution. On *themselves*, and on the world as it actually is.

> "The real voyage of discovery consists not in seeking new landscapes, but in having new eyes." — Marcel Proust

I felt I had to learn to see the world from that kind of perspective. To strip away the illusion of safety and discover what remains. What can I truly count on?

## The Rules of the Road

I carry what fits on my back. I sleep where shelter presents itself — in albergues, under bridges, in the homes of strangers who become friends. I eat simply. I write when the day's walking is done and my feet are soaking in whatever stream or basin I can find.

Every euro I receive and every euro I spend is recorded openly in the [financial ledger](/finances) of this site. There is no sponsorship, no hidden fund. If someone offers *osettai* — the traditional alms that pilgrims receive in Japan — I accept with gratitude and record it honestly.

This is not asceticism for its own sake. It is an experiment in trust.

## What This Site Is

These dispatches are my trail journal. They are letters sent from the road to anyone who wants to walk alongside me in spirit. I write about what I see, what I think, what I'm learning about the strange relationship between human beings and material security.

If something here resonates, [share it with a friend](/essays). If you'd like to support the walk, you can [offer osettai](/donate). And if you have questions, the [FAQ](/faq) is a good place to start.

The road begins at the end.

*— Henro, Cape Finisterre, October 2026*
