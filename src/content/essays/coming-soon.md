The most natural question people ask me is:

> Why are you doing this trip?

I've always asked the question backwards: Why are you going to school? Why are you taking a university course? Why are you working that job? For those things, you might be satisfied with commonplace answers, such as: "I don't know"; "Because I have to"; "Because I like it"; "It's my calling"; "It's what makes me fulfilled"; "It puts bread on the table"; etc. 

We've all heard it: you have to do well at school so you can get better grades, so you can enter a better course, so you can get a higher paying job, so you can live a comfortable life.

My 97-year old grandma always says "I just want to see you thrive". To her, thriving means having a stable, well-paying job, earning at least 1500€ a month, paying my bills on time, being able to dine out at least once a week, having a nice house or farm somewhere, and a family of my own.

To me, that may or may not be thriving. It is certainly <strong>not</strong> thriving if I am doing a job that drains me just because it's the only way I know how to support my family.

To me, thriving means being able to get out of a situation that I don't like <em>while</em> being able to support my family. In other words, thriving means <em>freedom</em>, or autonomy. And that takes three things: experience, knowledge and creativity.

So I'm going on this trip to fuel these three things - especially the last one, financial creativity. I'm going to discover how many different ways I can find to sustain a human life on an ever-changing environment, with very little room for possessions. This, I believe, is true wealth - not what you have accumulated, but the ability to acquire what you need, when you need it. That's where real security lies.

Robert Kiyosaki borrowed in his book *Rich Dad Poor Dad* a certain **definition of "wealth":**

> If you stopped working today, how long could you survive?  (...) Wealth is the ability to survive X number of days forward (while maintaining your desired standard of living).

In his book, this is mostly connected with how your assets generate money so that you don't have to work - your money works for you. When you can afford to completely not work anymore (i.e. retire), then you really are a wealthy person.

To me, this goes wider than that. Your skills are your greatest asset. When your skills allow you to change your lifestyle to whatever you want it to be, regardless of what you are leaving behind - be it your job, your career, your education, etc. - then you are truly wealthy. You have what everyone in the world wants. You are not tied to that paycheck because you know that your life is not dependent on it. If you can easily find another job, or start your own business, or live from your investments, or join a community that synergizes with the way you want to live... If you have that confidence, then you don't need to fear *anything*. Nobody can control you. Not your boss, not the government, not your spouse. Because you have the ability to survive forward, on your own terms. You can finally become a powerful force for *good* in the world, instead of being another piece in a machine that you hate but that you can't escape. It takes a strong foundation of security in one's life to go out and fight for a better world. 

So today, if you feel you do not have this freedom in your life, I invite you to check in with yourself: what would it require to take back that power? Are you doing a job that you hate, only to pay the bills? What will happen if you don't? Are you *sure* you know the answer to that? It might turn out that you're afraid of something you don't even know what it is... 



*Your pilgrim*
