There is a saying in English: 

> "You can't have your cake and eat it".

When I left home to start this pilgrimage, I packed with me a small container of almond butter that I had made at home. It was going to be an "emergency craving" for whenever I felt very low on motivation. I knew it was going to run out eventually, so I tried to make it last as long as possible. I wanted to 'have' it. 

I used it sometimes, sparingly, but whenever I had other options that could substitute it, I would use those alternatives instead. The result? I walked several days, maybe weeks, without even thinking about it, almost forgetting it existed inside my pack. Nevertheless, for the most part I still had my "emergency cravings" satisfied.

One day I was eating a slice of pizza that someone had offered me from their leftovers. Looking at the pizza, I realized something: I treat my cravings and my base nutrition very differently. I would never save this pizza out of fear of not finding more pizza later. I treat all nutritive food as the same kind of good, which I call simply *'food'*, and if I don't get more of one thing I'll get more of another. But I do save my chocolate, because chocolate satisfies a different kind of need than 'food' does - it is a *'sweet'*, not a 'food'.



There are two types of goods in this world: renewable and non-renewable. Renewable goods are the ones you know how to get more of. You don't have to worry about spending them. Non-renewable ones are the opposite: once they're gone, they're gone - you can't rely on them.

This distinction hasn't got anything to do with fossil fuels. It's about where you are right now and what you can do. It's a personal distinction. For example, if I'm in Europe and my partner brings home a deluxe chocolate from Dubai after a business trip, I might see that as non-renewable, as once it's gone, we can't buy anymore in Europe. At least, that was until they started selling Dubai chocolates in Europe en-masse 2 years ago. From that moment on, they became renewable, because now I can buy more whenever I want. Unless I don't earn enough money, in which case I *can't* buy them whenever I want, so they're again non-renewable.

What I did with the almond butter is a normal instinct to have when it comes to non-renewable goods: we see them as limited, so we tend to save them until the very last moment. 

But here's the thing I realized looking at that pizza: Once the non-renewable goods are gone, you'll have no choice but to do without them, and rely solely on the renewable ones. And if you believe you *can* do without them later, then you can also do without them *now*.

In the end, only renewable goods can sustain your needs. If you don't have access to a systematic source of renewable goods, you're screwed. But if you *do* have renewable goods, then you don't need to worry about spending your non-renewables, because you have a renewable alternative. So, you can spend and enjoy them freely, without restriction. 



**The key is to understand how you're categorizing things in your life.**

Because, you see, that Dubai chocolate wasn't a unique item. It satisfies my need for sweetness and comfort food, but it isn't the only item that does that. Before my partner came from Dubai, I was meeting my need just fine. That Dubai chocolate is part of a category of 'sweets', and that category is renewable. Just as that pizza was part of the category of 'food', and I don't worry about eating it.



**The lesson:**

When I realized I was restricting myself from eating the almond butter by using renewable alternatives instead, I realized the almond butter was part of a renewable category. Then I was able to really enjoy that almond butter freely, without worrying, because I knew that the underlying need would still get satisfied afterward. Thus, I removed my attachment to that particular object.

In general, I believe all of our needs can be met with renewable categories of goods. On the road, I might not be able to rely on a single item being renewable, but I know that wherever I go there will be sweets and food. I just need to learn what categories are important for me.

So yes, you can't have a cake and eat it. But that's not a problem if you know where the bakery is.

***PS:** Despite this lesson, I still carry with me a tin of canned food at all times as emergency food. Even if food is renewable, it might take some time before I find the next meal. In fact, it could completely stop being renewable for some time! The truth is, these categories aren't fixed in place. All of this philosophy is just a tool to help you navigate life. You wouldn't trust a compass to steer your ship for you! You still have to look at the sea ahead. Just like that, these categories can be used as a compass, but always keep your eyes on the changing reality of the world.*
