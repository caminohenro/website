FAQ

Q: Where are you from?
A: Henro was born in Buxán, Fisterra (Spain) on the 28th August 2026. Henro is the result of Vasco meeting Kasa and joining forces. 

Q: How many kilometers/days to Japan?
A: Who knows what the road holds for me? That's part of the beauty of travel. 
That said, I like to imagine it will take me the same time as my partner to finish her bachelor's degree. That means 3 years, starting September 2026. Of course, the road is unpredictable, especially with whatever bureaucracy for moving between countries, which might delay me a long time. 

Q: Why are you doing this trip?
A: This is probably the most important question. I chose to do the longest trip I could possibly imagine because I knew I could never save enough money to do it, so I would really have to depend on my ability to get by on the road. I would have to become a nomad. 
Most people look for security in their job or career, but I felt that real security couldn't be found there. It had to be something deeper. I thought of wild animals and ancient nomads. Their security is entirely dependent on their adaptations to the environment around them. I felt I had to learn to see the world from that kind of perspective, instead of hiding behind an illusion of safety in the form of a job where everything in my life depends on a single person who pays me a salary. 
Once I have that kind of security, I can do whatever I want in life - I can work a 9-to-5, or start a business, or become a monk or hermit - because even if everything goes wrong, I know what I can really count on.

Q: Why Japan / What's in Japan?
A: The initial idea was to just cross the largest distance on foot I could possibly conceive of, which was to cross the entire Eurasian continent to the tip of China. Then I figured, since I'm going to the tip of China, I might as well take a boat and go to Japan. That's why. 
But since joining with Kasa, an additional goal came in, which is to return Kasa to his original home in Shikoku Island and complete the 88-temples pilgrimage with him.

Q: What about money? How do you fund your trip?
A: The short answer is "I don't" - the trip is supposed to fund itself. From a combination of people's hospitality, me using my creativity to seek opportunities, and God doing His work behind the scenes, my quest is to find out how we can start trusting and stop being afraid of being without money.

Q: How many languages do you speak?
A: At the moment I would say I speak 6 languages at different levels: Fluent Portuguese and English, very good Spanish, moderate Japanese and German, and apparently I can also manage with French. I've also started learning Russian, but I can't maintain a proper conversation yet.

Q: What do your parents/partner think about this?
A: My parents I can't really say for sure, but it's cool. My partner is really excited and loves the fact that I'm doing it, and she's also concerned about how she will be able to meet her need for connection. We're both confident love will find a way.

Q: What route will you take to Japan? How will you cross the ocean?
A: The idea is to stay close to the pilgrimage routes that exist along the way. Plan A is to use the road to Jerusalem and go from there through Iran into India and China. Plan B is to go up from Turkey into Georgia and around the Caspian sea. 
As for the ocean, well, I suppose Jesus will teach me how to walk on water *facepalm*

Q: You walk with these sandals?! 🩴
A: I'm using Earth Runners sandals that I ordered from Germany before setting out. They are made for running outdoors, in nature, and apparently they last a long time. 
I wanted shoes that didn't cost me an eternity to take off and on every time I wanted to swim in a river along the way. With these sandals, I never have to worry about getting socks wet, and I can go in the river with them on. It's very handy. I also have boots as backup, and sometimes I also walk barefoot. The more I get used to walking barefoot, the less money I will need to spend on shoes.
