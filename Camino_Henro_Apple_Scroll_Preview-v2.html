Scroll No. 01 • Stage 1: Iberia • 8 Min Read

On the Weight of a Rucksack & the Gift of the Road

By Henro (遍路)
一

Before a pilgrim takes the first step eastward toward the rising sun, every object in his possession is put on trial by gravity. In a stationary life, an extra coat, a third pair of boots, or an unused ledger costs nothing to keep in the corner of a room. On the road from Portugal to Japan, every gram demands metabolic tribute.

同行二人 (Dōgyō Ninin) — Walking together, two as one. On the trail, material security is never found in the weight of the vault, but in the rhythm of the step and the unexpected fruit offered along the way.

We are taught to equate security with accumulation—an ever-higher wall of assets between ourselves and contingency. Yet the paradox of the long-distance pilgrim is that security increases in direct proportion to what one is capable of relinquishing while remaining whole.

問答 • Dialogues on the Way

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions asked along the pilgrimage
問答
問 Why walk from Portugal to Japan?
Portugal ("Where the Land Ends and the Sea Begins") and Japan ("The Land of the Rising Sun") form the two extremities of the Eurasian continent, uniting the Camino de Santiago with the 88 Temples of Shikoku.
問 What is the meaning of the Henro stamp crest?
It combines the Shikoku bamboo sedge hat (Sugegasa) and Sanskrit seed syllable with the Camino scallop shell and Cross of Saint James, resting over two crossed pilgrim staves.
問 How are essays published while walking?
Essays are written in plain Markdown from a mobile phone or public library computer along the trail, automatically unfurling onto this website within sixty seconds.
